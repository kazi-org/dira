Pressing `n` on a staged entry says **the regex misread me, this was never a
decision**. It disposes of the capture, and the entry is deleted.

It does not mean *we considered that and decided against it*. That sentence
describes a real decision, and a real decision goes to `y`.

## The two meanings, and why only one of them is a keystroke

The mockup has one word, "Reject", standing in front of two unrelated acts:

1. **The capture was wrong.** `dira sniff` matched decision *shape* — an
   imperative followed by a contrast (`dec-0021`) — in a sentence that announced
   nothing. There is no decision here and never was. Roughly one staged entry in
   four is this (`docs/decisions-pending/E2-L1-report.md` §3.3).
2. **The option was refused.** Something genuine was weighed and turned down.

Only (1) is a statement about the *record*. (2) is a statement about the *world*,
and the ledger already has a place for it — a `rejected` decision, which
`entry.schema.json` calls "first-class and permanent: the substrate `dira check`
reads to stop relitigation."

The thing that separates them is not taste, it is what each one can be written
from. (1) needs nothing the human did not already supply by pressing a key. (2)
needs the reasoning, and the human pressing `n` at 2am supplied none.

## What the schema forces, quoted

`entry.schema.json`, in the decision branch, on any state other than `staged`:

> `"required": ["alternatives"]`, `"properties": { "alternatives": { "minItems": 1 } }` —
> *"Once a decision leaves `staged`, a human has stood behind it and the roads not
> taken are part of what they stood behind. `alternatives: []` satisfied the letter
> of the old rule while asserting that there were none — which is exactly the
> assertion this field exists to prevent."*

`rejected` is one of those states. So the "record a rejected decision" reading is
not merely undesirable — from a bare keystroke it is **unwritable**, because the
regex tier writes zero alternatives and a keystroke supplies none. The only
moves available are to invent a `why_not` (forbidden by `dec-0003`, named as
invention by `dec-0019`) or to write `alternatives: []` (refused by `dec-0021`,
and by the Go validator before it).

**Where the ≥1 alternative comes from, for the reading that does store a
`rejected` decision.** From the semantic tier, via `y`. `dec-0022` settled that
confirming a regex-staged entry hands it to extraction rather than accepting it;
that mechanism is verdict-agnostic, and a decision that landed on "we are not
doing this" needs exactly the same extraction as one that landed on "we are". So
meaning (2) already has a keystroke, and it is not `n`. This decision adds no
mechanism to serve it — it points at the one that exists.

## What deletion makes unrecoverable, and what mitigates it

Deleted: the entry's `title` and its `source.excerpt` — dira's copy of the
transcript fragment the match was made on — plus the `created` stamp and the
session id. `dira sniff` runs from the `Stop` hook, so a staged file can be
minutes old and was very likely never committed; git will not have it.

Four things bound that loss, and the fourth is the honest one.

1. **Single-level undo.** `u` restores the last disposition, byte-identical,
   compared by sha256 (E2-L4-T5). The keystroke that is destructive is the one
   the design already required an undo for.
2. **The excerpt is a quotation, not the original.** The transcript it was cut
   from is still on disk and still in the session that produced it. What is lost
   is dira's pointer to the evidence, not the evidence.
3. **Nothing citable is destroyed.** A staged decision is never enforcement
   substrate — `dira check` cannot read one, `dira why` will not chain through
   one, and no brief renders it. Deleting one removes a proposal, not a record.
4. **The reject signal is lost, and no undo brings it back.** Out-of-sample
   precision was measured at ≈73% by hand over four transcripts. A ledger that
   kept its rejects would be a standing true-negative corpus for tuning
   `internal/sniff`; deletion means every future measurement is another manual
   pass. This is a real cost of the reading and it is accepted, not solved — the
   second alternative above carries the `revisit_if` that would reopen it.

## The one thing this reading breaks, stated rather than discovered later

`internal/sniff` dedupes candidates by title **against the ledger**
(`existingTitles` in `stage.go`): a sentence is staged once because a staged entry
with that title is already there. Deleting the entry removes the only record that
the sentence was ever seen, and the `Stop` hook fires against a transcript that
only grows — so **the next `Stop` in the same session re-stages the sentence the
human just rejected**.

That is not hypothetical and it is not this lane's file to fix; `docs/plan/lanes/E2.md`
scopes E2-L4 to the disposition flow and the dedupe lives in the capture tier.
It is recorded here, on the decision that causes it, so that whoever owns the fix
finds the reasoning rather than the symptom. The shape of the fix is a
session-scoped memory of what was rejected in *this* run — which is process
state, not ledger state, and therefore does not reopen any of the alternatives
above.

## What this costs the surface copy

Nothing in `docs/design/screens/s3-distill.html` has to change, but the register
does: the key is "not a decision", not "reject". `Reject` names the option;
`n` disposes of the capture. Getting that word right on the card is the cheapest
place to stop the two meanings collapsing back together.
