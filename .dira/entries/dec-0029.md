`int-0002` says a hook invocation must return in well under 100ms on a cold cache.
This entry is not that number — the number is unchanged — it is what dira is
allowed to conclude from measuring it.

## What is measured: the shipped binary, spawned

`internal/perf` builds `./cmd/dira` and runs it as a subprocess, twenty times per
measurement, over the 200-entry fixture with `.dira/cache` removed before every
cold run. Never `go run`, which times the compiler. Never an in-process call.

E1-L5 measured the read path in process and reported **17.3ms warm and 61.2ms
cold** over the same fixture. Both figures are honest and neither is a hook's
cost: they begin at `local.Open` and a hook begins at `execve`. Everything
between — process creation, the dynamic loader, Go runtime start, package init,
the config read, the cache open — was measured by nothing in this repository
before this lane. `E1-L6-T5`'s phase attribution put that excluded prefix at
**60.8% of one cold invocation** on the maintainer's machine, with the phases
summing to **98.0% of the same run's measured minimum**. An attribution that does
not add up is a story; this one reconciles inside 1% against an acceptance line
that asked for 10%.

## Two statistics, two different jobs

**The MEDIAN carries every absolute assertion.** It is the statistic `int-0002`
was written against, and since `min <= median` by construction, a ceiling read off
the minimum fires strictly less often than one read off the median. That is not a
theoretical hazard here: it was done once, to a live 60ms budget, and argued for
as robustness.

**The MINIMUM decides whether the machine is entitled to a verdict at all.** A
best sample comfortably inside the ceiling is proof the code CAN meet it, so a
median outside it is a statement about the scheduler rather than about dira.
Reporting that as a regression is the error this repo keeps making in new
costumes: a check announcing a verdict it lacked the evidence to reach.

Three absolute budgets now carry both statistics for exactly this reason —
`internal/index/latency_test.go`'s warm-cache budget, `internal/ledger/fixture`'s
full-read budget, and `hooks/pre-commit`'s own spawn-floor check, which measures
`dira version` and prints **NOT RUN TO A VERDICT** rather than red when that floor
eats half the tightest ceiling. `internal/perf` reports its minimum today and
asserts nothing with it; making that a verdict is a task with its own two-sided
proof, not a constant.

**Reporting the minimum is not asserting against it, and the two must not be
unified.**

## The CI policy: a dedicated runner, and budget.go untouched

The perf job runs on a GitHub-hosted runner per measurement — a fresh VM
executing one job and nothing else — with `-p 1`, in a job of its own, on both
`ubuntu-latest` and `macos-latest`, asserting `internal/perf/budget.go` exactly as
written. No multiplier, no percentile substitution, not `continue-on-error`. That
is `internal/index/latency_test.go`'s lesson applied at the job level: **the answer
to contention is to remove the contention, not to loosen the assertion.** What was
rejected is in the alternatives above, because the alternative to writing it down
is re-litigating it after the first red run.

Both platforms, because darwin-arm64 is `int-0002`'s primary user platform and a
latency budget certified only on linux is certified where nobody runs the hook.

Run `31230739083` (2026-08-08), both legs green, `budget.go` unmodified:

| | ubuntu-latest | macos-latest |
|---|---|---|
| cold brief, median (asserted, 100ms) | 38.967 ms | 55.294 ms |
| cold brief, min (uncontended estimate) | 36.987 ms | 43.245 ms |
| warm brief, median (asserted, 80ms) | 14.130 ms | 29.752 ms |

## What the measured distribution says, which answers dec-0026's revisit_if

`dec-0026` parked a percentile ceiling until a measured distribution existed. It
now does — **200 cold samples over 5 CI runs and 2 platforms** — and the answer is
that **a percentile ceiling still cannot be derived honestly**. macOS's pooled p90
is **111.1ms**, above the 100ms budget it would be enforcing, and the variance is
*between* runs rather than within them: four macOS runs sit well inside the
ceiling and the fifth has every sample shifted upward, including its minimum. A
within-run percentile cannot separate those two populations, so no choice of
percentile fixes it. The trigger has fired, been examined, and produced a refusal
rather than a threshold.

One macOS run in five did break the **median**, and it is recorded here because
the lane recorded it nowhere: run `30669750621` (2026-07-31), cold median
**111.073ms**, perf job red — seven minutes before `dec-0026` was written. Its
**minimum was 49.678ms** against a typical macOS minimum in the low 40s, so the
whole distribution had shifted: a degraded runner, not a slower dira, and exactly
the case the minimum-as-discriminator exists to name.

## A correction to dec-0026, which is a correction and not a reversal

`dec-0026`'s second rejected alternative gave as its reason that "the median on CI
passes at roughly 95ms against a 100ms ceiling". **No CI log supports 95ms.** The
figure was a projection from an earlier task report, explicitly labelled an
estimate there, carried into a decision entry as though it had been measured. Real
cold medians observed on the perf job run from the high 30s on ubuntu to 111.073ms
on the one degraded macOS run; `dec-0026` now carries the harvested list in its own
correction note.

The conclusion it reached is unchanged and in fact stronger. At n=20 the maximum
IS one observation with no averaging behind it, so a 191ms sample grades the
scheduler; medians of roughly 39ms against a 100ms budget make that argument more
comfortable, not less. **Claiming a number no log supports is the defect this
lane's whole discipline is against**, and it was committed inside a decision entry
about measurement, which is why it is corrected in the entry that codifies the
discipline rather than quietly fixed.

## Where a local run cannot judge, and why that is not a bypass

`/usr/bin/true` — a binary that does nothing — and `dira version` — an invocation
that opens no ledger — are the floor under every budget here. On the maintainer's
machine, running a dozen parallel sessions, `dira version` has been measured at a
**95-107ms median through this harness**, already over `warmBudget`'s 80ms, and
during T5's work at a 247.50ms median in a window where `/usr/bin/true` alone took
97.57ms. A budget verdict taken there is a report on the machine.

So `hooks/pre-commit` measures the floor before reporting a red, and prints **NOT
RUN TO A VERDICT, and NOT treated as red** when it reaches half the tightest
ceiling. The test is not "is the floor over the ceiling" but "does the floor leave
enough of the ceiling to measure dira in". Below half, the red stands and the
commit fails. **CI is the authority; a local number is a diagnosis.**

## The socket claim, and the honest size of it (cst-0004)

The same spawned run that carries the latency claim is the one traced for sockets,
so both claims are made about one execution rather than two. `strace` observes the
run on the linux leg and the test fails on any `socket`, `connect` or `socketcall`
attributed to it. Three things make that worth something: a positive control that
provably opens a socket is traced first on every run, so an unattached tracer makes
the test SKIP rather than certify dira by silence; the control is checked untraced
first, so a broken control and a broken tracer are separated; and an empty trace
file is a failure, because it means tracing did not happen.

**On darwin the assertion skips and says so.** SIP blocks dtrace, and the darwin
path is fail-safe by construction: if the script is wrong or the probe cannot
attach, the control shows no socket and the test skips. `internal/perf/NETWORK.md`
publishes that, along with what the method does not reach — one command, one
ledger, one execution; not `dira ui`, which correctly listens; not a linkage check,
which would be red on a correct binary today because `cmd/dira` legitimately links
`net`, `net/http` and `crypto/tls` through the localhost UI. **Linkage is not
evidence of abstinence. The runtime trace is.** The linux leg is required to run
that test to a verdict — CI errors if it skips there — and it passed on run
`31230739083`, with the control's own sockets observed in the same trace.

## What was refused, recorded as refusals

- **No daemon, no resident process, no warm-up trick.** The spawn floor is the
  largest single phase in the attribution and a pre-forked helper would remove most
  of it. `int-0002` forbids them and the budget exists to protect the property they
  would violate.
- **No network cache**, and nothing on the measured path reaches the network
  (`cst-0004`).
- **No edit to `internal/perf/budget.go`.** Raising a number there is a decision
  about `int-0002`, not a fix for a red run.
- **`ix.Close()` is not skipped on the happy path**, though it happens after stdout
  is already written and would look free. The work moves to the next process's
  recovery — which is the hook invocation this budget is about — and "fast because
  it left a mess for the next run" is the trick the budget exists to prevent.
- **The parsed entries are not memoised between the cold build and the render.** It
  would save about 4ms and would put a value that did not come from a file read on
  the rendering path, trading `internal/index`'s structural invariant for a careful
  one.

What was *not* refused is the one pass that decided nothing: a cold build read
every entry file twice, once to hash and once to parse, and the hash existed only
to be compared against cached rows that do not exist on an empty cache. Removing it
took `sync.list` from an 11.212ms minimum to **0.457ms**, and the removed work
benchmarked on its own at **60.60ms/op against 0.70ms/op**. It is asserted by
counting calls rather than by timing them, because a 5ms effect is not resolvable
on a machine whose spawn floor moves between 250ms and 2.2s — and a whole-binary
A/B attempted there is reported as a failure of the instrument, not as a result.

## int-0002 is met, and is not superseded

On the median, on a healthy runner, 38.967ms and 55.294ms against 100ms is "well
under", not a near miss. No `supersedes` edge, no clause changed, no constant
touched. This entry changes what the measurement is allowed to say, not what it is
measured against.
