`dira sniff` may stage **decisions** and nothing else. `alternatives` gains
`minItems: 1` in the schema, waived only while `state: staged`.

## What this fixes that was actually broken

`alternatives: []` validated against `entry.schema.json` and was **rejected** by the Go
validator. Two halves of the same rule disagreed, and neither test noticed, because each
was only ever run against the other's happy path. That is the fifth instance this month
of a check agreeing with itself rather than with reality — see `docs/lore.md` L-0001.

## A weakness this exposed in dec-0003's reasoning

`dec-0003` argues for no model client partly by asserting that "regex reliably catches
decision *language*", and names `"let's go with"` and `"we're not doing"` as its
examples. Measured across this machine's stored sessions, those two phrases appear in
**12 and 2 transcripts respectively**, against roughly **1,100 each** for `"instead of"`
and `"rather than"`. In the single session E2-L1 measured in full, both named phrases
occur **zero** times.

**This does not overturn `dec-0003` — it strengthens its conclusion and falsifies its
illustration.** If the phrases regex is supposed to reliably catch are two orders of
magnitude rarer than the ones people actually use, then the regex tier is *weaker* than
`dec-0003` assumed, and the semantic tier it argues for is more necessary rather than
less. The decision stands; the example in it should not be quoted as evidence again.

The corrected reading: regex catches decision *shape* — an imperative followed by a
contrast — not a fixed vocabulary. `"rather than"` and `"instead of"` are the real
handles, and both are far too common to use alone, which is precisely why the tier may
only stage.
