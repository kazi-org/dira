Pressing `y` on a **regex-staged** decision hands it to the semantic tier for
extraction. It does not move the entry to `accepted`.

## The contradiction this resolves

`dec-0021` gives `alternatives` `minItems: 1` the moment a decision leaves `staged`.
`dira sniff` writes no alternatives and no body — `dec-0003` forbids the regex tier from
asserting a `why_not` it cannot know, and `dec-0019` calls inventing one what it is.

So the two rules met and produced an impossible state: `docs/design.md` §10 promises
*"You say 'confirm.' That is your entire clerical workload"*, and doing exactly that
would have written a schema-invalid entry. **The promise was made before the schema rule
existed and neither knew about the other.**

## Why promotion rather than waiver

The keystroke survives. What changes is what it means: `y` is the human saying *this is
a real decision, go get its reasoning*, not *this record is complete*. The entry stays
`staged` — visibly, with its extraction pending — until something can supply the
rejected alternative that makes it enforceable.

That keeps the one property the enforcement surface depends on: **every non-staged
decision in the ledger can be cited back at a plan.** A ledger where some accepted
decisions carry no rejection is one where `dira check` is silently weaker than it looks,
and nothing on the surface would say which entries were which.

## What this costs, stated plainly

Confirm is no longer instant. There is now a gap between the keystroke and the entry
being usable, and if the semantic tier is unavailable that gap is unbounded. The queue
therefore has to show *pending extraction* as a state a human can see, or entries will
silently pile up in `staged` looking rejected.

`docs/design.md` §10 and §4 are corrected in the same change, because a promise that
survived its own falsification once will survive it again.
