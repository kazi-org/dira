## The decision

`brief.max_tokens` counts **dira tokens**, defined in `internal/brief/tokens.go`
and computed in the binary with no table, no network and no model:

> One per line, plus, for each line, the greater of its **word pieces** — a run
> of letters counts one per four characters, a run of digits or punctuation one
> per character, and a non-ASCII rune one per UTF-8 byte — and its **byte
> floor**, one per three bytes of that line with its whitespace collapsed.

Two independent estimates, and the larger wins. Prose that packs into few words
is caught by the byte floor; a dense line of ids, dates and punctuation is
caught by the word pieces. Neither alone is safe: a word count is blind to how
long the words are, and a byte count prices `dec-0002  accepted 2026-07-29` like
a sentence.

## Why the error direction is the whole point

An estimator that is right on average is wrong low half the time, and cst-0001
is not an average — it is a ceiling the binary asserts it did not cross. So this
one is built to be wrong high:

| text | dira tokens | what a cl100k-family BPE typically spends |
|---|---|---|
| ` constitutional` | 4 | 1 |
| `dec-0002` | 6 | 4 |
| `1,500` | 5 | 3 |

Measured over this repository's own brief the whole document counts 1,271 dira
tokens for 3,637 bytes, which is **1.40x** the four-characters-per-token rule of
thumb. That margin is what is paid for not shipping a vocabulary, and it is
stated rather than hidden: a reader who sets `max_tokens = 1500` gets a brief
that a real tokenizer would put nearer 900, and the direction of that error is
deliberate.

## What this buys the cap

The counter is **deterministic** — the same bytes always count the same, so a
golden test over a brief is stable and two machines agree — and it is
**subadditive over blocks that end in a newline**, which is what lets the
renderer fill the ceiling one measured block at a time and still be under it
when the blocks are concatenated. `TestTokensIsSubadditiveOverBlocks` pins that
property, because the fill silently depends on it.

## What it does not claim

It is not a tokenizer and it is not accurate. dira has nothing to measure
against — that is dec-0003's consequence, accepted here rather than
worked around — so the honest claim is bounded: the count is never below what
any tokenizer would need for the same text (one per whitespace-delimited word,
one per line), and it is typically well above it. `dira brief` reports the
number it counted on stderr whenever it drops anything, so the estimate is
observable rather than internal.
