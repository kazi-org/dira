The pitch to any repo with history is "import what you already have." kazi has 77+
ADRs; that corpus is the first real test.

The risk is precise: ADRs record *the choice*, and usually record context, but they
rarely record the **rejected alternatives with their why_nots** — which is the field
`dira check` reads and the field that makes `dira why` worth reading (dec-0003 makes
the same point about regex capture). An import that yields 77 entries with empty
`alternatives` arrays produces a ledger that cannot enforce anything: the ADR pile,
reindexed, with a nicer front end. That is a bad first impression at exactly the
moment a user is deciding whether dira is real.

Three options:

1. **Index, don't import.** Treat existing ADRs as read-only referenced documents —
   `dira why` can cite and link them without claiming them as entries. Preserves
   honesty about what is enforceable. Weakest immediate payoff.
2. **Semantic import via the skill.** Let the live session read each ADR and extract
   alternatives where they exist, marking entries `staged` where they don't. Better
   fidelity, but 77 staged entries is a triage queue nobody will finish.
3. **Import only what is cited.** Import an ADR the first time something references
   it. Lazy, ordered by real relevance, no queue — but the corpus stays mostly dark,
   so it does not answer "what did we decide" retrospectively.

Leaning toward (1) plus (3): index everything for retrieval, promote to a real entry
on first citation. Needs validating against the actual kazi corpus before committing.

## Measured 2026-07-31 — the premise above is false, and the real blocker is elsewhere

Full method and controls in `docs/decisions-pending/qst-0003-experiment.md`. Measured
over kazi's 80 ADRs (81 files, one is the index):

| | measured |
|---|---|
| ADRs carrying at least one rejected alternative **with a reason** | **63 / 80** |
| alternatives extractable | **218** |
| ...carrying a real `why_not`, after hand-check | **218 / 218** |
| ...bare name, no reason | **0** |
| `why_not` length, median | **23 words** |
| ...carrying a `revisit_if` | **2 / 218** |
| ADRs with no alternative at all | **17 / 80** |

This entry predicted "77 entries with empty `alternatives` arrays." That is simply not
what the corpus contains. The triage objection to option (2) — a queue nobody finishes
— is answered by the split: 63 import with real content and 17 have nothing to
extract, and a 17-item queue is finishable.

**The blocker is the `option` label, not the `why_not`.** `dec-0014` makes matching
lexical and in-binary, so the label is the only handle `dira check` has. Against four
real relitigation attempts plus a negative control, the imported corpus cited correctly
**2 of 4**, and loosening the threshold to catch the other two made it cite the *wrong*
record for both and refuse the negative control seven ways. **21 of 218 labels carry two
or fewer matchable words, and they are the load-bearing ones** — `Go`, `Rust`, `Redis`,
`Git refs`, `MCP-only.`, `LLM-as-judge for completion.` The `why_not` under `Go` is
three rich sentences; the handle is one word that is also an English verb.

**The evidence supports option (2), semantic import** — not the (1)+(3) leaning above,
which was reasoned from the falsified premise. (2) is also the only option that reaches
the two ADRs whose rejections sit in prose rather than under a heading: a regex importer
gets 61, a semantic one gets 63.

**Two requirements no option above states**, both falling out of the citation test: the
importer must **expand short option labels at extraction time**, which is free then and
expensive afterwards — it is the difference between an entry that can refuse something
and one that is merely readable; and it must **report `revisit_if` as absent** rather
than let its absence be discovered when `check` first fires.

## Why this stays open

**n=1, and the sample is not neutral.** kazi's ADRs were written by the same person
building dira, using one heading string in 54 of 80 files. That is the best possible
case for an importer and the worst possible evidence for "any repo with history."
Measuring two or three unrelated public ADR corpora is the cheapest thing that would
move this answer, and the import pitch should not ship before it.

Also unestablished: that a live session *extracts* as accurately as a regex did. What
was measured is what the corpus contains, not whether a model reliably pulls it out —
and a semantic importer that hallucinates a `why_not` is worse than no import at all.
