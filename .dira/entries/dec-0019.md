`docs/design/screens/s1-decision.html` renders **four** alternative cards under
"Alternatives on record — 4". Three are refusals. The fourth is the *upheld* option —
"Go — one static binary, sub-100 ms cold start" — with its own tag, its own one-line
summary and its own grounds paragraph. `entry.schema.json` models `alternatives` as the
roads **not** taken: `why_not` is required, and it is documented as "the actual reason" a
road was rejected. The chosen option therefore has no field to live in.

**The upheld card is dropped. The ruling carries it.** The chain's matching `✓` row goes
with it, for the same reason and one more: `dira why` emits `✗` lines and never a `✓`,
and `internal/why` is one producer behind both renderers. A web chain showing a mark the
terminal cannot print would make the "same output `dira why` prints" claim marketing.

## The rule this establishes

> The renderer derives presentation from recorded prose. It never invents a field, and
> where nothing can be derived it renders nothing.

Both halves are load-bearing, and the second one is what makes the first honest.

The **derive** half settles the other gap in the same block. `dec-0017` makes the
one-line ground load-bearing — "the summary line carries the grounds, not just the
title" — and there is no `summary` field either. There does not need to be one: the
first sentence of `why_not` is the one-line ground, and the rest of `why_not` is the
detail. Nothing is stored, nothing is duplicated, and a reader scanning twenty summaries
is reading the author's own opening sentence rather than an editor's paraphrase.

The **render nothing** half settles the upheld card, because there is no prose to derive
it from. And that absence is not a hole in the schema — it is the schema being right.
The real `dec-0001` records the grounds the mockup invented a card for:

> Rust — *"Equivalent start-up and single-binary story to Go, with better guarantees, but
> slower to write and a smaller pool of contributors for an OSS tool that wants drive-by
> PRs. Go's stdlib covers everything dira needs (files, JSON/YAML, an embedded HTTP
> server for `dira ui`, SQLite via a single dependency)."*

The mockup's upheld card reads "The standard library already covers files, JSON and YAML,
an embedded HTTP server for `dira ui`, and SQLite through a single dependency." That is
the same sentence, **moved out of the refusal it answers**. The case for the chosen road
is made where it belongs: inside the reasons the other roads lost. Adding a field would
have created a second place to put it, and a second place is how the two drift.

## Why the struck-refusal device survives, argued rather than assumed

E6-L1 flagged this option as the one that would break the device `DESIGN.md` defends in
its r3 → r4 record, and asked for the argument rather than the default. Here it is.

**What r3 → r4 actually defends is the strike with its grounds beneath**, not the strike
beside an upheld card. The rejected fix was "collapse the alternatives into a scannable
comparison list", and the stated reason is that it "would destroy the struck-through
refusal with its grounds beneath". Every refusal keeps both. `dec-0017`'s account is the
same and mentions no upheld card at all: "the summary line carries the grounds, not just
the title."

**The counterweight is not lost; it gets bigger.** A strike is legible against unstruck
type, and after this change the nearest unstruck type is the `h1.ruling` at up to 52px —
by the same r3 → r4 record, "one element clearly wins" the frame at a 3.15:1 type ratio
against the grounds. That paragraph is the *anchor fix* r3 → r4 chose in place of the
comparison list, and its direction was explicitly "reducing element count and
strengthening the anchor". Removing a 16.5px card that restates the 52px anchor is that
same move, taken once more. Inside each card the strike also sits directly above its own
unstruck grounds, so the contrast exists per-row and not only per-page.

**The same record already flags the card as slop-adjacent.** r3 → r4 notes the upheld
card's coloured left border as "adjacent to the 'left-border accent stripe' slop tell",
kept only because the state was also carried in text (WEB.md 12). That defence needs a
state to carry. There is no such state in the data.

**The section stops lying about its own count.** "Alternatives on record — 4" counts
three refusals plus a card that is not an alternative. It becomes 3, and the number now
matches both the file and `dira why`.

## What this costs, stated rather than buried

1. **`dec-0017`'s above-six rule loses its named anchor.** It said: above six alternatives
   emit only the upheld one open. It now reads **above six, emit every `<details>`
   closed** — which is `dec-0017`'s own description of the outcome ("the page becomes an
   index that expands in place") with the exception removed. At or below six, everything
   stays open exactly as before. Nothing else about `dec-0017` moves.
2. **A twenty-alternative page opens with no prose expanded.** The ruling and the because
   are still above it at full size, so the page still argues before it indexes.
3. **`.alt.upheld` becomes dead CSS** and is removed from `screens/decision.css` with its
   comment. Dead rules named after a deleted element are an invitation to restore it.
4. **Three mockups change**, not one: `s1-decision.html`, `s1-decision-long.html` and
   `s1-decision-withheld.html` are one page with three payloads, and leaving any of them
   with a card the template cannot emit would leave the disagreement in place.
   `docs/design/landing/index.html` also refers to the refused/upheld contrast in its
   copy and is **not** changed here — it is another lane's surface and it is not a route
   `dira ui` serves.

## The check that keeps this true

`fixture-check.mjs` printed this as a FINDING on every run. The finding is replaced by an
assertion: no screen may carry an `upheld` state tag or a `✓` mark in an alternatives or
chain block, because those are the two shapes of "a card the ledger cannot produce". It
is verified in both directions — restoring one card fails the gate.
