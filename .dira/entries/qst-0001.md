`kazi/.dira/entries/int-0001.md` carries `derives_from: sire:int-0002`. That ref is
committed into a public repository, and `sire` is private. So a stranger cloning
kazi sees a dangling pointer into a ledger they cannot read.

Three candidate answers, none obviously right:

1. **Opaque refs are fine.** The public ledger shows `sire:int-0002 (unresolved)`.
   Honest, leaks only the existence of a private parent and its id. Simple, but the
   orphan-drift flag can no longer distinguish "no parent" from "private parent I
   cannot see" for an outside contributor.
2. **Public alias entries.** The private parent gets a redacted public stub with a
   safe title. Resolves cleanly for everyone, but now the same intent exists twice
   and the two copies drift — and deciding what is "safe" is a judgment call made
   under time pressure on every write.
3. **Strip on publish.** Upward edges to private parents are omitted from what lands
   in the public repo. Maximally safe, but it makes every public repo ledger look
   like orphan work, which breaks the drift signal exactly where it is most useful.

Answering this requires deciding who the public `.dira/` is *for*. If it is for
contributors, resolution matters. If it is documentation of the maintainer's
reasoning that contributors merely read, (1) is sufficient and cheapest.

Blocking: the workspace tier cannot ship until this is settled.
