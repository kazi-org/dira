Pressing `y` on a regex-staged decision writes exactly two things: `confirmed_by:
human`, and a bumped `updated`. Every other frontmatter field is left byte-identical,
and `state` stays `staged`.

A staged decision with `confirmed_by: human` is the record of a human having stood
behind the *capture* while its reasoning is still missing. A staged decision without
it is one nobody has looked at yet. That is the whole discriminator the queue needs,
and the schema already carries it.

## The collision, with both its sources

**`dira sniff` writes no `alternatives` and no body.** An entry as the regex tier
actually writes it carries `id`, `kind: decision`, a truncated `title`, `state:
staged`, `created`, and `source{hook, session, excerpt, tier: regex}` — nothing else
(`docs/decisions-pending/E2-L1-report.md` §4 prints one verbatim, and `dec-0021`
explains why the tier may not do more).

**`entry.schema.json` requires `minItems: 1` on `alternatives` the moment a decision
leaves `staged`** (`dec-0021`), waived only while it is staged:

> Once a decision leaves `staged`, a human has stood behind it and the roads not
> taken are part of what they stood behind. `alternatives: []` satisfied the letter
> of the old rule while asserting that there were none — which is exactly the
> assertion this field exists to prevent.

So a bare state flip on `y` produces a file the published schema rejects. Zero
alternatives in, ≥1 required out, and a keystroke supplies none.

## Why this resolution and not a state change

`dec-0022` already ruled that `y` means *this is a real decision, go get its
reasoning* and that the entry stays `staged` until something can supply the rejected
alternative. What it did not say is what lands on disk, and "stays staged" read
literally would mean `y` writes nothing at all — leaving the queue unable to tell a
confirmed entry from an untouched one on the next run.

`confirmed_by` closes that gap without touching the schema. Its own description in
`entry.schema.json` is *"Who dispositioned this entry"*, which is precisely what the
keystroke supplies and precisely what the regex tier could not. The field is a free
string with no rule in the schema coupling it to a state, so `state: staged` with
`confirmed_by: human` validates today, unchanged.

**The schema is not amended by this decision.** `schema/entry.schema.json` is
untouched, no new fixture is added under `schema/testdata/invalid/`, and
`go test ./schema` grades this entry against the contract as published. That is
stated rather than left to inference, because E2-L4-T2's `acc:` makes the fixture
conditional on an amendment and a reader should not go looking for one.

## What survives, and what it costs

The keystroke survives whole: one byte, one write, no editor, no prompt. What does
not survive is the reading of `docs/design.md` §10 in which confirming *completes*
the record — the human's clerical workload is still the single keystroke, but the
entry is not finished when the key is released, and §10 already says so as of
`dec-0022`.

The other property this preserves is the one `dec-0024` had to spend: nothing is
deleted, so `internal/sniff`'s title dedupe against the ledger still sees the
sentence and the next `Stop` does not re-stage it. The rejection loop `dec-0024`
records is specific to the reject path and this decision neither fixes nor worsens
it; the session-scoped memory named there stays that lane's to build.

## A clause this falsifies, reported rather than quietly satisfied

`docs/plan/lanes/E2.md` §E2-L4 and `docs/plan/tasks/E2-L4.md` T5 both assert that
after a scripted `y`, `n`, `e` *"no entry in the ledger has `state: staged`"*. Under
this ruling that is unreachable, and it was already unreachable under `dec-0022`
before this entry existed: `y` leaves its entry staged by design, and `e` edits a
body without supplying an alternative, so two of the three cards remain `staged`
whatever the keystroke loop does.

The clause is testing the right thing with the wrong predicate. What it wants to
assert is that the queue *empties* — that nothing is left awaiting a human — and the
predicate that says so under this ruling is: **no entry in the ledger is `staged`
without `confirmed_by`**. That is a real red→green test, it fails against a loop that
silently skips a card, and it is true of a ledger where every card was disposed.

Those files are not this task's to edit (`files:` here is this entry and
`docs/coverage.md`), so the correction is recorded here for whoever owns E2-L4-T5
rather than applied to a task file behind its claimant's back.
