Three segments, sequenced — and they are different *customers*, not competing
pricing models:

1. **Now — free OSS forever:** CLI, hooks, the Claude Code skill, and `dira ui`.
2. **v1 apps — one-off purchase:** iOS + desktop bundle. Data stays on-device or in
   the user's own GitHub. Privacy-as-product is a genuine strength here rather than
   a constraint, and it costs nothing to serve: no ops, no on-call, no security
   surface. Plan for pay-once-per-major-version, which is how sustainable indie
   apps square "one-off" against "updates forever."
3. **When teams pull for it, not before — per-seat subscription:** shared review
   queues, members without GitHub, SSO, org dashboard, notifications. Real ongoing
   cost, real ongoing value, subscription-shaped.

The honest caveats on the one-off lane, recorded so they are not rediscovered as
surprises: one-off revenue is a decaying curve per release, and App Store discovery
is brutal without the OSS funnel doing the marketing work.

The ordering is itself the strategy: the paid apps fund the OSS credibly ("buying
the app supports kazi and dira") instead of the OSS reading as bait. cst-0004 holds
throughout — no tier of this model is allowed to make the hosted service necessary.
