**Lane:** `docs/plan/lanes/E2.md` §E2-L4. **Prompt:** `docs/plan/prompts/L2-E2-L4.md`.
**Status: not started.** Unlike the five task files already in this directory, nothing

**Bound:** 8 tasks (≤8 per the L2 prompt).

**Waves:** `[T1, T3]` → `[T2]` → `[T4]` → `[T5, T6]` → `[T7]` → `[T8]`.

## What is already true, so no task re-derives it

- **`dira sniff` landed and stages decisions only.** A staged entry as the regex tier
  actually writes it carries `id`, `kind: decision`, a truncated `title`, `state: staged`,
  `created`, and `source{hook, session, excerpt, tier: regex}` — **no body, no
  `alternatives`, no `confirmed_by`** (`docs/decisions-pending/E2-L1-report.md` §4, and
  `dec-0021`). Nothing exists to dispose those entries, so they accumulate with no exit.
- **Roughly one in four staged entries is a reject.** Out-of-sample precision measured
  **≈73%** over four real transcripts (`E2-L1-report.md` §3.3). The flow's cost on `n` is
  therefore as load-bearing as its cost on `y`, which is why T5 asserts the reject path's
  byte count and not only the confirm path's.
- **`dec-0021` put `minItems: 1` on `alternatives` the moment a decision leaves
  `staged`**, waived only while staged. The schema's own text names this lane as one of
  the two things that fill the field in ("Tier 2 and the disposition flow fill this in").
  Two of the three keystrokes therefore collide with the schema, and T1 and T2 exist to
  settle that collision **before** the keymap is written, not after.
- **`dec-0019` governs the card.** *The renderer derives presentation from recorded
  prose. It never invents a field, and where nothing can be derived it renders nothing.*
  `docs/design/screens/s3-distill.html` shows a because, a struck alternative with its
  grounds, and a `mirrors to adr/0011` line. A regex-staged entry has none of those. T8
  renders what is there and nothing else.
- **The codec preserves presentation on write** (`internal/ledger/style.go`, pinned by
  `TestEditingOneFieldRewritesOnlyThatField`), so "frontmatter byte-identical apart from
  `updated`" is reachable rather than aspirational.

## Boundaries this file inherits and does not cross

- **CLI only.** `docs/design/screens/s3-distill.html` is E6's surface. The transitions
  live in `internal/distill` with no terminal in their signatures so E6 drives the same
  package; no web surface is built here.
- **The ADR mirror (`dec-0009`, `mirror.adr`, the schema's `adr` field) is not in this
  lane.** `docs/plan/lanes/E2.md` flags it to the lead rather than absorbing it. Until a
  lead adds it, confirming an entry writes one file: the entry. T8 asserts the card emits
  no `mirrors to adr/…` line for the same reason.
- **`dira log` is E1's.** A disposition is one write through E1's write path (`dec-0002`),
  not a second writer.

### E2-L4-T1 — Record what `N` disposes: the capture, or the option

Two unrelated acts hide behind the mockup's "Reject": *the regex misread me, this was
never a decision*, and *we considered that and decided against it*. The schema has one
word for both and `additionalProperties: false` leaves no field to tell them apart. Get
this wrong and a regex misfire is quoted back at the user by `dira check` three weeks
later as their own recorded reasoning — worse than losing the capture.

New evidence the L2 prompt did not have: `dec-0021` makes a *rejected decision* require
≥1 `alternatives[]` with a non-empty `why_not`, and the regex tier writes none. So the
"record a rejected decision" reading is not merely undesirable, it is unwritable from a
keystroke without inventing a `why_not` — which `dec-0003` forbids and `dec-0019` names
as invention. The entry must engage that, not assume it.

- **files:** `.dira/entries/dec-00NN.md` (new, the next free `dec-` id — `dec-0022` as of
  this writing), `docs/coverage.md` (the disposition row the new accepted decision
  creates — L-0010)
- [x] **acc:** a new `.dira/entries/dec-*.md` names **both** meanings and records which one
  `N` performs; it validates against `schema/entry.schema.json` (`go test ./schema`
  passes, which validates every file in `.dira/entries/`); it carries ≥3 `alternatives`,
  each with a non-empty `why_not`, and a `derives_from` edge to `dec-0003`; it quotes
  `entry.schema.json`'s `minItems: 1` conditional and says what the chosen reading does
  about it — a reading that stores a `rejected` decision must name where its ≥1
  alternative comes from, and a reading that deletes must name what becomes
  unrecoverable and what mitigates it; `grep -c "minItems" .dira/entries/dec-00NN.md`
  returns ≥1; and `python3 scripts/coverage.py` exits 0.
- **depends_on:** none
- **red today:** the entry does not exist; `grep -rl "the regex misread me" .dira/entries/`
  returns nothing.

### E2-L4-T2 — Settle what `Y` writes when the staged decision has no alternatives

The lane's own `acc:` says the confirmed entry gains `confirmed_by: human` **and a state
legal for its kind**. For a decision the legal non-staged states are `accepted`,
`rejected` and `superseded`, and `entry.schema.json` requires ≥1 `alternatives` for every
one of them. `dira sniff` writes zero. So a bare state flip on `y` produces a file the
published schema rejects, and `docs/design.md` §10's *"You say 'confirm.' That is your
entire clerical workload"* is, for regex-tier entries, currently false.

This is a decision with real alternatives — confirm captures an alternative out of band
(a fake `$EDITOR` in the test, so the scripted three bytes still hold); confirm is
refused with one line until an alternative exists; or the schema changes again — and it
must be recorded before T4 implements anything.

- **files:** `.dira/entries/dec-00NN+1.md` (the `dec-` id after T1's), `docs/coverage.md`
- [x] **acc:** a new `.dira/entries/dec-*.md` names the collision with both its sources —
  that `dira sniff` writes no `alternatives` and no body
  (`docs/decisions-pending/E2-L1-report.md` §4) and that `entry.schema.json` requires
  `minItems: 1` once a decision leaves `staged` (`dec-0021`) — records which resolution
  wins, carries ≥3 `alternatives` each with a non-empty `why_not` and a `derives_from`
  edge to `dec-0021`, and states in one line what the resolution costs the one-keystroke
  claim in `docs/design.md` §10; it validates (`go test ./schema` passes); and **if** the
  resolution amends `schema/entry.schema.json`, a new fixture under
  `schema/testdata/invalid/` fails validation for the new rule and `go test ./schema`
  fails when that fixture is moved to `valid/` (red→green in both directions);
  `python3 scripts/coverage.py` exits 0.
- **verifies:** lane `acc:` — *"the confirmed entry gains `confirmed_by: human` and a
  state legal for its kind"*.
- **depends_on:** E2-L4-T1
- **red today:** no entry mentions the collision; `grep -rl "dec-0021" .dira/entries/`
  returns only `dec-0021.md` itself.

### E2-L4-T5 — Build the one-keystroke loop, with single-level undo

One card actionable at a time; a single byte disposes it. No Enter, no confirmation
prompt, no menu. `y` confirm, `n` discard, `e` edit, `q` quit leaving the rest staged,
`u` undo the last disposition. The undo is not optional if T1's `n` is destructive: the
queue is driven by muscle memory, ≈27% of what it holds is a reject
(`E2-L1-report.md` §3.3), and a staged file created since the last commit is not in git.

- **files:** `internal/distill/tui.go`, `internal/distill/tui_test.go`
- [ ] **acc:** `go test ./internal/distill -run TestKeystrokes` passes on a temp ledger of 3
  staged entries: a scripted stdin of exactly the three bytes `y`, `n`, `e` disposes all
  three and the ledger then holds zero `staged` entries; the driver is given no other
  input bytes, so a loop that requires a newline fails; stdout between cards contains no
  second prompt matching `[y/n]` or `confirm?`, asserted by pattern; `u` after an `n`
  restores that entry to `staged` with its file byte-identical to before the discard,
  compared by sha256; `q` after one disposition leaves exactly the remaining 2 staged and
  exits 0; an unrecognised byte is ignored without advancing the queue; and the `n` path
  consumes exactly one byte and performs exactly one ledger mutation — no prompt, no
  editor, no second read — asserted by a stdin reader that records how many bytes it was
  asked for.
- **verifies:** lane `acc:` — *"a scripted stdin of exactly the three bytes `y`, `n`, `e`
  disposes all three, after which no entry in the ledger has `state: staged`"* and
  *"stdout contains no second confirmation prompt between cards"*.
- **depends_on:** E2-L4-T3, E2-L4-T4
- **red today:** the package does not exist; `dira distill` is an unknown command.

`updated`.

### E2-L4-T7 — Handle the non-interactive and empty cases without hanging

`dira distill` will run from scripts, from CI, and with a piped stdin. A raw-mode read on
a non-TTY that blocks forever is the bug that makes a tool untrustworthy in exactly the
automated setting dira lives in. An empty queue is the common case on most days and must
cost one line, not a screen.

- **files:** `internal/distill/session.go`, `internal/distill/session_test.go`

## Not decomposed further

No task here felt too large to converge in one kazi run, so the stop rule does not apply
and no L3 exists. Two notes on where the line was drawn.

**T1 and T2 are two tasks, not one.** They settle two different collisions — what `n`
means, and what `y` can legally write — and each is a real decision with its own
alternatives. Folding them into one entry would have put two rulings behind one
`supersede`, and would have made two tasks own one file.

**Command registration rides with T8 rather than standing alone.** A registered command
that refuses to run would make E0-L1-T3's help predicate green against a non-functional
entry, which is the failure that lane deliberately avoided; the registry line lands with
the surface it advertises.

## What this lane does not settle, and says so rather than absorbing

The one-keystroke promise in `docs/design.md` §10 does not survive `dec-0021` unamended
for regex-tier entries — T2 records which way it is resolved, but whichever way it goes,
either the promise or the schema moves. That is a product call visible in the design copy
and in `.agents/product-marketing.md`, and this file flags it rather than choosing on the
lead's behalf.
