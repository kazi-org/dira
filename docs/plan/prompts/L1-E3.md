**Read `docs/plan/prompts/_L1-CONTRACT.md` first.** It defines lane structure, the

**Your epic:** E3 — The enforcer
**L0 `acc:` line:** see the E3 section of `docs/plan.md`, verbatim. Your lanes must
collectively satisfy it; no single lane needs to.

## Scope

`dira check`, `dira supersede`, constraint inheritance, and `revisit_if` surfacing.

## Specific things to get right

- **This is the epic that makes dira more than a notebook.** `dira check` runs before
  predicates are drafted and exits non-zero on contradiction, citing the entry, the
  rejected alternative, and its `why_not`.
- **`revisit_if` is what stops the check being merely obstructive.** It distinguishes
  a closed door from a locked one. Surface it in every conflict message.
- **Rejected decisions are first-class and permanent** — they are the substrate the
  check reads. A design that only reads accepted decisions misses the point entirely.
- **Constraints inherit downward** through `derives_from`, across ledger boundaries.
  When a check must cite a *private* constraint in a public context it cites the
  **ref only** (`me:cst-0002`), never the text (`cst-0003` — a violation here is a
  security bug, not a UX bug).
- Supersession must actually change enforcement: the superseded entry stops being
  enforced, its superseder starts. Plan a red-to-green test for exactly this.
- Consider how the check avoids false positives. A check that cries wolf gets
  disabled within a week, and then `int-0001` fails for mechanical reasons.
